CI (GitHub Actions) runs the tests after every phase; the Lune suite was not run locally.

## Phase 1: playtest blockers

- **How-to-play placement.** It no longer opens in the match. A player who hasn't seen it gets it in
  the hub before their first match (centered, dim backdrop; the backdrop is a plain Frame and takes
  no input). If a match starts while it is open, or the "?" button opens it in a match, it docks at
  the left edge without a backdrop, so the board, bench and shop stay usable. "?" toggles it.
  **Decision:** hub-first instead of a side panel during the first Preparation (simpler, and the
  first match starts with a creep round that needs no shopping anyway).
- **Rejoin mid-match.** CombatService keeps this round's fight payload per player. Every client fires
  `RequestMatchSync` once its screens have started; for a participant the server resends board and
  shop, and during Combat the fight, which CombatPlayback already plays from the current moment
  (earlier events applied without their one-shot effects). A "You're back! ... ends in Xs" banner
  shows for 5 s. Sync requests are rate-limited (one per 2 s per player).
- **Leaving never stalls.** Checked by reading: the hub queue is rebuilt from the current players
  every poll (a leaver drops out; the countdown cancels if the queue empties), phase timers are
  plain clocks that don't wait on anyone, and every send/state update skips players who aren't
  there. A leaver stays in the match (leaver grace rounds) and can rejoin. No change needed.

Open questions:
- Rejoining can only be tested on a live server (Studio local tests can't reconnect a client).

## Phase 2: bots that feel fair

- `MatchConfig.botDifficulty` = `"easy"` (default for Play-pad fills) or `"normal"` (the old
  policies). Easy bots (`Bot.easyPolicies`): no rerolls, ignore traits and bonds, no copy chasing,
  buy XP only in 10-15% of Preparations, put about half their units on a random free cell, and
  field one unit under their level cap (`fieldsFewer`). Normal bots make exactly the same choices
  as before (random draws only happen when a policy asks for them).
- `Bot.newPlayerPolicy` (sim only): buys random affordable offers, buys XP in 20% of
  Preparations, fields its highest-star units in random order on random cells, sometimes sells a
  random bench unit when the bench is full.
- `sim/difficulty.luau`, 6-player matches, seat 1 = new player:

  | bots | matches | new player top 4 | avg place |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | easy (first try: XP 40%, misplace 30%, copies on) | 20 | 10% | 5.60 |
  | easy (XP 10-15%, misplace 50%, no copies) | 30 | 23% | 5.20 |
  | easy (+ one unit under cap) | 30 | 53% | 4.50 |
  | **easy (final)** | **80** | **49%** | **4.55** |
  | normal | 40 | 5% | 5.78 |
  | mixed (3 easy + 2 normal) | 40 | 38% | 4.78 |

  In the mixed table normal bots average place 1.6-2.7 and easy bots 3.8-4.5.
- Bots play Preparation in steps: each waits 12-35% of the Preparation, shops, then places one
  unit every 0.6-1.4 s. `BotService.finishPreparation` completes every bot before the boards lock.
  **Decision:** opponents' boards aren't visible during Preparation anyway, so this mostly matters
  for anything that later shows other boards; it costs nothing.

## Phase 4: phones (by reading)

- Every hover has a tap equivalent (card "i" button, tap on trait symbols, tap to inspect units).
  No keyboard-only controls outside the dev tools.
- At a phone's usual UI scale (~0.7) small buttons were under the touch guideline. `UI.touchSize`
  grows them on touch devices: "?" and practice Leave to 48 reference px (~34 pt), the card "i"
  button to 34, the feedback stars and buttons to 48. **Decision:** 48 reference px rather than a
  full 44 pt (63 px), which would break the layout.
- Text: long strings (top bar, bench hint, intro) fit the 1100-px reference width; names truncate.

## Phase 5: performance (by reading)

No clear wins found; nothing changed. Checked:
- Server: no per-frame loops (hub poll at its interval, ErrorLog flush every 30 s); remotes send
  small per-player tables on change; fights are sent once per round; all per-player tables are
  cleaned on leave or at the match end (queue, sync rate limit, feedback, payloads, bot jobs).
- Client: only the player's own board and fight are drawn; per-round views and effects are cleared
  (CombatPlayback.clear); per-effect frame connections disconnect.
- Left as is (small): ImpactEffects keeps its RenderStepped connection after the first effect even
  when idle (an empty update per frame; VFX code is frozen); TopBar and MatchIntro read attributes
  every frame (cheap); UnitTooltip raycasts every frame with a mouse (one filtered ray).
